What the MVP planner calculates

A minimum viable product is a small complete way to test whether something is useful. The planner treats your available hours as a constraint, and helps you choose the work that belongs inside it.

Weekly hours × weeks − reserve = time for planned work

Each feature has a low and high estimate. The app adds included features, applies a 1.5× learning allowance if selected, and adds 2–3 hours to investigate the problem, 3–4 hours to test, and 1–2 hours to make the first release. Reserve stays outside those tasks.

If the upper total fits, the route has room. If only the low total fits, it is tight. If even the low total is over budget, reduce the slice or extend the window. “Fit my time” removes optional features by priority, together with dependents, until the upper total fits or only core work remains.

What the estimates mean

These are editable starting ranges authored for this tool, for small web prototypes using familiar, existing components. They are not measured delivery benchmarks. Replace them with estimates from your own tasks as soon as you can.

Large amounts of content, custom infrastructure, regulated workflows, app-store review, provider approval, procurement and ongoing operations are outside these ranges. A production release needs the security, accessibility, reliability and support work appropriate to what it does. “Core” means core to this prototype pattern, not universally mandatory.

Why the experiment comes first

The workflow draws on the practice of meeting early users directly, described in Paul Graham’s “Do Things that Don’t Scale”. The templates, effort model and suggested experiments here are original planning aids. A successful interview or tiny test is evidence to examine, not a market forecast.

A few useful answers.

Is this MVP planner free?

Yes. You can plan, save projects in this browser, export files and use the offline edition without an account or a payment. Hosting, software providers and other costs for the project you choose to build are separate.

Can it turn any idea into a business plan?

It is a first-release planner for small web projects and manually delivered services. The brief assistant recognizes a limited set of English keywords and time phrases, then suggests an editable starting pattern. It does not research a market, call an AI service, produce a financial forecast or predict demand. Add custom features and estimates for work the pattern does not cover.

Will my projects be private?

The app saves projects on your device and does not send their contents to a server. A link you choose to share includes its stated project fields. JSON backups and Markdown briefs include your notes and milestone progress. Keep your own backups: clearing browser data can remove local projects.

Does the installed version work offline?

After the online app reports “Ready for offline use,” the planner and these three playbooks are cached for use without a connection. Installation and cache availability depend on the browser. The downloaded HTML edition contains the planner, styles and fonts in one file; links to other pages still need a connection.

What if the smallest version still does not fit?

The app keeps the core flow and tells you that it needs more time. You can extend the window, revise the individual estimates if you have evidence, choose a smaller pattern, or run the interview/sketch experiment before writing software. It never removes necessary work just to show a green result.
