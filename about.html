03 / ORIGIN

LOGGED 03:14 A.M.

It started at 3:14 a.m.

AXON began as a design question: what would an automation product look like if its interface treated auditability as the central feature rather than a settings-page afterthought? The 3:14 a.m. framing is a narrative device for that question, not a claim about a deployed AXON incident.

The showcase turns the idea into an inspectable interface: sense, reason, act, and explain. The page demonstrates the visual system, the interaction design, and a simulated trace. It does not provide the autonomous-agent backend depicted by the concept, and nothing on it is for sale.

04 / PRINCIPLES

TOLERANCES, NOT SLOGANS

  1. P·01

    Autonomy needs an audit trail.

    An agent you can't inspect is a liability, not a teammate. AXON's interface study makes a reasoning trace the primary object.READ THE DESIGN NOTE →

  2. P·02

    Guardrails are a feature, not friction.

    Scoped permissions and human approval gates are design requirements for any automation entrusted with consequential actions.READ THE DESIGN NOTE →

  3. P·03

    Latency is a moral issue.

    Latency budgets should be explicit and measured in a real implementation. AXON uses a sub-40ms hand-off as a design target, not a production benchmark.READ THE DESIGN NOTE →
